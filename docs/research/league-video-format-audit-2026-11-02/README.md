## What similarity means for this project

The useful part of Midbeast’s presentation is sustained observation of one named pro in a match, with English commentary, a readable game clock, a usable battlefield view and a consistent HUD. A facecam is optional. It becomes a problem only where it hides required evidence. A video lasting 30–50 minutes does not establish that it covers a whole match.

The snapshots and captions distinguish broad game progression from teaching concentrated on a small game interval. For example, the Hidon Zeka sample is still near game 06:57 at video 26:16; the Bonks+ Chovy sample is near game 05:01 at about video 30:10. Shok’s Aurora sample reaches game 26:30 near video 25:02 and captions discuss the ending at 27:26–27:40. These are sparse observations, not measured playback-speed or cut rates.

The visual inspection found meaningful variation *within* a channel: Shok moves his facecam from bottom right in the Aurora video to bottom left in the Akali video; Coach Curtis switches from a spectator review of Doinb to a player-POV review of Rookie. One shared channel crop template would therefore be unsafe.

## Admission rules for the next collection

1. Pin one pro, one match and one upload version. Verify the pro account separately; an opponent mentioned in the title is insufficient.
2. Keep solo queue and professional competition in separate cohorts. Establish the patch; historical examples prove presentation style only.
3. Require at least 1080p for the primary intake. A 30 fps source can support slow state tracking, but needs separate validation for action timing.
4. Sample the opening, middle and ending before admission. Require evidence of meaningful game progression and the ending, or explicitly label partial coverage.
5. Calibrate each layout span. Mask facecams, promotions, chat, drawings and slides. An obscured HP number remains unknown; it cannot be reconstructed from commentary.
6. Record source-video time and visible game time separately. Split at rewinds, repeats, jumps, speed changes and non-game segments. Count a repeated event once.
7. Check caption/speech availability, then assess actual audio separately when that modality is used. This audit did not listen to or grade sound quality.
8. Deduplicate the underlying match across commentators and clean replay uploads before measuring behavior across games.

The Doxa sample is a concrete duplication warning: its [commentary upload](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=16YRhdoBCg8) shows LOLREC branding and matches the visible Lucian/Syndra player and roster context of [this clean replay](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fnc2BRTCcf0). Treat them as a strong same-match candidate pending match-ID confirmation. The clean version exposes the selected-unit panel hidden by the commentary facecam. Two upload URLs must not automatically become two games.

## Evidence and limits

Every sampled ID was checked against the existing P1 inventory, the current video metadata and an exported English auto-caption transcript. The machine-readable [audit.json](audit.json) records the findings, metadata properties, transcript hashes and limitations. The raw metadata and temporary caption exports are local research evidence; no media download command was used.

No upload has passed a complete visual extraction or audio acceptance test through this audit. A reliable next pilot is Shok Aurora, Virkayu Canyon Gwen and Doxa Chovy Lucian, each with its own layout calibration and temporal segmentation. Use the LS/Phylaris examples to test rewinds and 30 fps handling; keep lane-only coaching as a separately labeled set.
